# Trusted Skills — the aspirational README

*Private. For the two of us. This is not a roadmap — no tasks, no dates. It is a
picture of what becomes reachable when a capable agent stands on a floor it can
trust: the apps we haven't built, the people who couldn't build them before, and
what the whole thing could grow into.*

## The floor, not the ceiling

Most software is a ceiling. Someone decides what it does, ships it, and freezes
it; you live under what they imagined. A trusted skill is the opposite. It is a
floor — a dependable place to stand — and its whole point is that you can build
upward from it, further than we can see from here.

For forty years, systems that sense the world and respond to it belonged to
institutions, because only institutions had the expertise to build them correctly
and the machines to run them. The aspiration is easy to state and large to mean:
hand that power to a person. A student. A shopkeeper. A field scientist. Someone
who can describe what they want, in their own words, and lean on tested machinery
for the parts that are hard to get right.

## A fourth beat: growth

The public page ends at three beats, and rightly so — it describes an app you can
use today. This page is about the fourth.

Provision is the first sentence you speak. Trust is the first time you rely on
what comes back. Habit is coming back tomorrow. **Growth** is everything after:
the app becomes yours. You extend it — a new rule, a new check, a new component
the substrate keeps safe. You compose it with others into something no single
skill anticipated. You come to understand the floor you are standing on. Over
months, the thing you built and the person who built it both get larger.
Shrink-wrapped software cannot do this; it is the same on the last day as the
first. A trusted skill is a starting point, not a destination.

## A gallery of futures

None of these exist yet. All of them are reachable from the floor.

**The commons that compounds.** A student writes a new component — a strategy, a
filter, a check — and it passes its tests. The next student builds on it without
knowing how it works, the way you use a library without reading its source. The
trusted skill for a domain is not a fixed thing we ship; it is a garden that grows
as people plant in it, and the tests are the fence that keeps the garden honest.

**The lab that composes a pipeline by talking.** A researcher screens a million
molecules, docks the survivors against a target, and — this is the part the
substrate guards — spends the expensive simulation only on the few that earn it.
She wires screen → dock → simulate by describing it, not by writing a distributed
pipeline. Offices inside offices; the costly step gated behind a join; the whole
thing built in an afternoon by someone who is a chemist, not a systems programmer.

**The shopkeeper's operations brain.** It watches orders, weather, and foot
traffic, and each morning tells her what to prepare and what to reorder — a small,
private, always-running system of the kind that used to require a team. She built
it by describing her shop.

**Systems that touch the physical world.** Sensing and responding began, for us,
with earthquakes and radiation — institution-scale work. The aspiration is that a
town, a school, a watershed can build its own: watch the sensors, decide when it
matters, warn the people who need warning. The stakes are real, which is exactly
why the floor must be trustworthy — ambition is only safe on tested ground.

**The tutor shaped for one child.** Not a course sold to millions, but a patient
tutor a parent shapes in conversation — one that adapts to the single learner in
front of it, and that the parent keeps adjusting as the child grows.

**The app that is yours.** You've had it two years. It has changed a dozen times
because you changed it. It fits you the way no purchased app ever could, because
it was never finished — it grew with you.

**Understanding, as a side effect.** A first-year builds something she loves — a
monitor for a hobby, a tool for a club. It works. Then she lifts the floorboards
and studies why: how the system knows it is done, how it takes a consistent
snapshot of a moving target. She learns distributed systems because she is
standing inside one she cares about. The tool taught her by being trustworthy
enough to trust, and transparent enough to study.

## What has to stay true

The aspiration is only as honest as the floor. Every future here is a bet that the
hard, invisible parts — the concurrency, the checks, the properties that are
catastrophic when wrong — hold up under a person who is not a systems expert and
should not have to be. Take the tested substrate away and this page is hype. Keep
it, and the page is a plan for handing real power to people who were never
supposed to have it. That is the whole wager: not that an agent can do anything,
but that an agent on trustworthy ground can let ordinary people build things that
matter — and keep growing them.
