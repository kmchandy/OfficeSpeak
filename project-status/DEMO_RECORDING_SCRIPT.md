# OfficeSpeak — screen-recording demo script

*Goal: a ~60–90 second video someone can watch passively, with zero
commitment, that gets them from "sounds interesting" to "let me try that."
Not a tutorial — a hook. Record once, reuse in every invite.*

---

## Before you hit record

- Have the **OfficeSpeak claude.ai Project already set up** (Stage 1's
  step 1, done ahead of time) — don't show or explain setup in this video at
  all. Setup is boring; GETTING_THE_FILES.md and README.md handle it in text.
  The video's only job is to make someone *want* to go read those.
- Open a **fresh chat inside the Project**, browser zoomed in (Cmd/Ctrl +
  a couple of times) so text is easily readable on a phone screen — assume
  most people will watch this on their phone, not a laptop.
- Close other tabs, mute notifications, hide bookmarks bar.
- Do **one full real run-through un-recorded first**, so you already know
  roughly how long Claude's response takes and what it says — this lets you
  record the real, second take smoothly, not fumbling live. The content
  should still be a genuine response, not scripted/faked.
- Have this exact paragraph ready to paste (don't type it live — paste it,
  so there's no typo-fumbling on camera):

  > Once each period my club gets a batch of market data, forecasts, and
  > news, plus our decisions from last period. Two analysts each read it and
  > recommend a plan — one value-investing, one chasing emerging
  > opportunities. A manager weighs both, proposes a plan, checks the taxes
  > and fees with an accountant, then writes the final plan to a file. Keep
  > our portfolio and history in one place, and handle one period at a time
  > so the books stay consistent.

- Have the correction ready too:

  > The accountant has to see what we currently hold — otherwise the tax
  > numbers are guesses. Taxes depend on what we paid for what we own.

---

## Shot list

**[0:00–0:08] Cold open — the pitch, on screen, before anything else happens**
On-screen text (or spoken, your choice — spoken is faster to produce and
feels more personal):

> "This is a real conversation. I'm not going to write any code. Watch."

Cut straight to the chat window — no logo screen, no slow intro.

**[0:08–0:14] Paste the description**
Paste the investment-club paragraph into the chat, hit send. Let the viewer
actually read it appear — don't rush this part, it's the "oh, that's just
plain English" moment.

**[0:14–0:16] Speed up through the "thinking"**
Cut or 3–4x speed through Claude's response generating. Nobody needs to
watch a spinner.

**[0:16–0:35] The team appears**
Slow back to normal speed right as the response lands. Scroll (smoothly, not
jerkily) through: the list of workers, the plain-English explanation of the
flow ("each period the office lets one batch in... the manager weighs
both..."). Pause for ~2 seconds on the **"Things I assumed —"** list — this
is the single most important beat in the whole video. If you can, briefly
zoom/highlight the third assumption ("the accountant works out the taxes
from the proposed plan alone — it does not look at what the club currently
holds").

**[0:35–0:42] The catch, spoken over the paused screen**

> "See that? It just told me a mistake it made — before I even asked."

**[0:42–0:50] Paste the correction**
Paste: *"The accountant has to see what we currently hold — otherwise the
tax numbers are guesses..."* Send it.

**[0:50–0:52] Speed up again through the response generating.**

**[0:52–1:05] The fix, on screen**
Normal speed as the revised explanation appears — let the viewer read the
"one new link... nothing else about the office changes" line in full.

**[1:05–1:15] The close — spoken, direct to camera or just narration over the
screen**

> "That's it. No code, about ten minutes, and I'll actually run it for real
> after. Want to try it?"

End card / caption: **link to the invite / README** and nothing else — no
logo animation, no music swell. One link, one clear next step.

---

## Editing notes

- **Target final length: 60–90 seconds.** If your real run-through was
  longer (it will be), cut ruthlessly — the two beats that must survive are
  "Things I assumed" catching its own mistake, and the before/after of the
  correction. Everything else is trimmable.
- **Captions on, sound optional.** Most people will watch with sound off.
  Burn in the two or three key lines of Claude's own text (the assumption,
  the correction, the fix) as captions even if you also narrate.
- **Don't clean up a rough real description if it happens.** If the actual
  take has Claude ask a clarifying question or hedge on something, that's
  fine to keep in (briefly) — it's more convincing as unscripted than a
  too-perfect take would be. Just don't let it run long.
- **Make a second short cut for a different audience** if you're inviting a
  parent/educator instead of a finance-curious person — same shot list,
  swap in the fractions-tutor description from `STAGE2_WALKTHROUGH.md`
  instead. Same beats, different domain.
