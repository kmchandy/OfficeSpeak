# investment_club — intended explanation for Pat

Think of it as a small team that acts every time relevant news or market
data comes in, and decides whether the club should buy, sell, or hold.

Who's on the team:
- **Warren** — your value analyst, argues from a value-investing angle.
- **Bill** — your new-opportunities analyst, argues from a growth angle.
- **Don** — the decision-maker, who makes the call.
- **Herb** — your tax-and-fees analyst; before Don finalizes a trade,
  Herb works out its taxes and fees.
- **Rachel** — the record-keeper; she holds the files: every argument,
  every decision, and the club's portfolio.

What happens when news arrives:
1. It goes to Warren, Bill, and Don.
2. Warren and Bill each check the current situation in Rachel's files and
   write their arguments, then hand them to Don. (They work separately —
   Bill doesn't see Warren's argument first.)
3. Don reads both, proposes a trade, and asks Herb about taxes and fees.
4. Herb answers; Don makes the final decision, files it, and Rachel
   updates the club's portfolio.
5. Only then does the office pick up the next piece of news.

Keeping score: on top of the club's real portfolio, Warren, Bill, and Don
each keep an imaginary portfolio — "what we'd hold if we'd followed only
my advice." Whoever's is doing best has the best track record, and Don
leans more on that person's advice over time.

Things I decided — tell me if any are wrong:
- one piece of news at a time, start to finish;
- Warren, Bill, and Don all look at what the club currently owns;
- Herb is asked once per decision, not in a back-and-forth;
- "trying to do better" just means the scorecards and trusting the better
  track record.
